# Alpaca paper trading and Gmail approvals

Settings → Paper trading & Gmail accepts Alpaca **paper** API key/secret and a Gmail app password. They are encrypted with the existing installation encryption key, never returned to the browser, never committed to GitHub. Blank fields preserve saved secrets. Keep the encryption key and database private together. This app runs locally; automation requires the computer/server to stay on.

Trading Division → Alpaca paper account shows broker-reported paper equity, cash, positions, order states and the email approval journal. Connecting Alpaca also makes supported US-stock market charts/research use Alpaca IEX bars. No silent fallback: failed Alpaca data is shown as a failure. News still uses GNews. IEX volume represents one exchange, not total-market volume. Historical data availability depends on the account plan.

Enable Gmail reply checking before requesting a proposal. The first inbox check starts from current UIDs; old messages are not approvals. A proposal is an experimental long-only US-stock bracket order with whole shares, cash coverage, maximum 1% configured risk, 2% daily equity-loss budget and a ten-minute expiry. One position/order at a time. It uses the highest qualifying research score (minimum 70), complete sourced research, an intraday ATR reference and at least 1.8 reward/risk. AI explains evidence; deterministic checks control all order parameters. This screening rule is not a validated profitable strategy.

Reply **YES** as the first line in the exact proposal thread to authorize its paper entry plus stop/target exits, or **NO** to decline. From must match the saved Gmail account and Gmail's inbound authentication assessment must show a passing Gmail DKIM signature. HTML-only replies and unverifiable messages do not authorize an order. The system does not use an AI model to interpret authorization.

After approval, fresh IEX trade and completed minute bar, market-open status, cash, empty account positions/open orders and current risk budget are checked again. A limit-entry bracket is sent only to the hard-coded https://paper-api.alpaca.markets endpoint. No live endpoint is configurable. Submissions have unique client order IDs. Network uncertainty blocks another proposal until reconciled; it never triggers a blind second submission. Submitted is not filled. Broker statuses are reconciled every 30 seconds when reply monitoring is enabled; fill/status updates are emailed. Stop orders can fill below stop prices even in simulation assumptions; bracket exits activate only after entry fill.

Opt-in schedules: one weekday proposal check at 09:45 America/Toronto, and one daily Manager paper report at 17:00. Reports include actual broker balances, positions, recent orders and journal; broker failure is stated. The existing Manager has a read-only paper-account tool and shared paper journal. Trading Division has manual proposal/report buttons. Tasks missed while the server is off are not claimed complete. Failed scheduled runs are recorded rather than repeatedly resending.

No credentials are available during development, so tests use mocked broker/SMTP/IMAP responses. After entering your keys, check the paper account and Gmail inbox, then test a real paper proposal during market hours. This connection has not been tested against your account yet. Historical strategy tests were negative after costs; neither AI reasoning nor paper execution establishes future profitability.
